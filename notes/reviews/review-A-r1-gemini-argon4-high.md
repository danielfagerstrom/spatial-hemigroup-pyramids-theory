# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** *Spatial scale space from hemigroup axioms: the theory of image pyramids* (112 pp.) and *Supplement* (46 pp.)  
**Author:** Daniel Fagerström

---

## 1. Overall Assessment and Recommendation

**Recommendation: Major Revision**

This manuscript develops a mathematical foundation for multiscale image pyramids by relaxing the full dilation-covariance axiom of linear scale-space theory to covariance under the powers $q^{\mathbb{Z}}$ of a single ratio $q > 1$. It delivers four substantive contributions: (i) a classification of symmetric and isotropic hemigroup scale spaces in $\mathbb{R}^d$ into three strictly nested strata corresponding to covariance groups $\{1\}$, $q^{\mathbb{Z}}$, and $(0,\infty)$, tying pyramid kernels to semi-selfdecomposable laws; (ii) an exactness criterion showing that a symmetric probability mask $w \neq \delta_0$ on a lattice is the octave step of a scale-continuous $q$-covariant family if and only if $w$ is infinitely divisible with a finite logarithmic moment, which rules out all finite-support probability masks; (iii) a selection theorem in $\mathbb{R}^d$ showing that while local-extremum counts fail to be monotone across pyramid rungs in $d \ge 2$, an implicit-Euler maximum principle required at every octave stage singles out the continuum Matérn pyramid (whose octave steps are Bessel potentials $(I - c^2 t^2 \Delta)^{-1}$) up to the periodic gauge group $\mathcal{G}_q$; and (iv) a structural obstruction proving that on a full-rank lattice in $d \ge 2$, no exact grid pyramid with a scalar dilation ratio $q$ can have a rotation-invariant kernel at any positive scale. The mathematics is sound and virtually every numbered statement is backed by a Lean 4 formalization from an explicit list of ten classical theorems taken as axioms. Nevertheless, a major revision is required for three reasons: first, several headline claims in the abstract, introduction, and discussion omit important scope qualifications that appear only later in the formal definitions or in Section 7.1; second, at 112 pages (plus 46 supplement pages), the manuscript is far too long and repetitive for a journal article, repeating proofs verbatim between $d = 1$ and $d \ge 1$ and transcribing low-level measure-theoretic boilerplate in a theorem-prover style; and third, the main paper is not structurally self-contained, as Section 4 relies on definitions given 40 pages later in Section 6, and the proof of the main Matérn pyramid theorem in $\mathbb{R}^d$ (Theorem 5.4) depends on theorems proved only in the supplement.

## 2. The Headline Claims Against What Is Proved

While the formal numbered statements in Sections 3–6 and the supplement are precise, the headline summaries in the Abstract (p. 1), Section 1 (pp. 2–4), Section 7 (pp. 104–105), and Section S2 (pp. S8, S34) overstate or blur the scope of several results by dropping qualifications present in the definitions.

1. **"no law of bounded support other than a point mass is a step of any family continuous in scale. So the finite masks of the classical pyramids are the octave steps of no family" (Abstract, p. 1; Contribution 1, p. 3; Discussion, p. 104; p. S8; p. S34; Proposition 3.21, p. 37; Proposition S.33, p. S35).**
   - *What is proved:* Proposition 3.21 proves that no *probability measure* of bounded support on $\mathbb{R}^d$ other than a point mass is infinitely divisible, and hence no such probability measure can be a step $\mu_{s,t}$ of a family of *probability measures* satisfying (I1) and (I2).
   - *What is missing from the headline:* By Definition 2.1(A4)–(A5) and Definition 3.1, a "family" in this paper is restricted from the outset to *nonnegative* smoothing operators (probability laws). In the classical pyramid literature, however, Burt and Adelson's 5-tap generating kernel $(c, b, a, b, c)$ with $b = 1/4$ and $c = 1/4 - a/2$ has *negative* outer weights whenever $a > 1/2$—including their preferred value $a = 0.6$ ($c = -0.05$) [2, pp. 533–534]. In Proposition S.33 (p. S35) the statement carefully qualifies *"to every five-tap mask of Burt's family that is a probability measure"*, and Section 7.1 (p. 105) notes that masks with a negative weight lie outside every statement because positivity is assumed. In the Abstract (p. 1), Contribution 1 (p. 3), and pp. S8 and S34, however, the claim that "the finite masks of the classical pyramids are the octave steps of no family" is stated without reminding the reader that a "family" means a *positivity-preserving* (probability-measure) family, and that classical masks with negative weights are excluded by positivity (A4) rather than by Proposition 3.21.

2. **"An octave step goes from a scale $t$ to $qt$... Up to the choice of the scale coordinate, it is the one isotropic family covariant under $q$ whose octave steps from every scale obey the maximum principle of an implicit Euler step" (Abstract, p. 1; Contribution 3, p. 3; Discussion, pp. 104–105; Proposition 5.18, p. 76; Theorem 5.19, p. 78).**
   - *What is proved:* Proposition 5.18(3) proves that a symmetric probability measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ satisfies the implicit-Euler maximum principle (Definition 5.17, p. 75: for every real $f \in \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^d)$, $u = \mu * f$ satisfies $u(x) \le f(x)$ at every weak local maximum $x$ of $u$) if and only if $1/\widehat{\mu}(\omega) = 1 + \omega \cdot C \omega$ for a positive semidefinite matrix $C$ (so $C = \tau I$, $\tau \ge 0$, when $\mu$ is rotation invariant). Theorem 5.19(2) then assumes an isotropic $q$-covariant family $(\mu_{s,t})$ *already in the canonical discrete gauge* $S_q t = qt$, and proves that if $\mu_{t, qt}$ has the principle for every $t \in [1, q)$, then $\Psi_t = F_q^1(\chi(t)\,\cdot)$ for some $\chi \in \mathcal{G}_q$ (where $\mathcal{G}_q$ is the group of increasing homeomorphisms of $(0,\infty)$ with $\chi(qt) = q\chi(t)$).
   - *Where the headline is imprecise:* In a general scale coordinate where $q$-covariance acts via an increasing bijection $S_q$ ($D_q \mu_{s,t} = \mu_{S_q s, S_q t}$), an octave step across a factor $q$ in spatial scale goes from $t$ to $S_q t$, not from $t$ to $qt$. Writing the octave step as going "from a scale $t$ to $qt$" already fixes the scale coordinate to the canonical discrete gauge $S_q t = qt$ up to the periodic gauge group $\mathcal{G}_q$ (Lemma 3.5(3)). Conversely, if one relabels the Matérn pyramid by an arbitrary increasing homeomorphism $\chi$ outside $\mathcal{G}_q$, then its step $\mu'_{t, qt}$ does *not* satisfy the maximum principle (by Theorem 5.19(3), only $\mu'_{t, S_q t}$ does). The headline in the Abstract and Contribution 3 conflates the two: either the octave step in an arbitrary scale coordinate should be described as $\mu_{t, S_q t}$ (making the family unique up to any increasing homeomorphism $\chi$ of $[0,\infty)$), or, if the octave step is written as $t \to qt$ in the canonical discrete gauge, the remaining gauge freedom should be stated as the $q$-periodic gauge group $\mathcal{G}_q$.

3. **"On a lattice, a symmetric mask other than a point mass has an exact grid pyramid if and only if it is infinitely divisible with a logarithmic moment" (Abstract, p. 1; Contribution 2, p. 3; Contribution 4, pp. 3–4; Discussion, p. 104; Proposition 4.5, p. 43; Proposition 6.6, p. 91).**
   - *What is proved:* For a symmetric *probability* law $w \neq \delta_0$ carried by a *full-rank* lattice $\Gamma \subset \mathbb{R}^d$, the ladder $w_n = D_{q^n}w$ is carried by a family $(\mu_{s,t})$ satisfying Definition 6.3(a)–(d) if and only if $w$ is infinitely divisible and $\int \log_+ |x|\,w(dx) < \infty$ (equivalently $\int \log_+ |x|\,\nu_w(dx) < \infty$).
   - *What is missing from the headline:* In Definition 4.4 (p. 42) and Definition 6.3 (p. 84), the word "mask" is defined to mean a *probability law* (nonnegative weights summing to $1$) on a full-rank lattice $\Gamma$. Because "mask" in image processing commonly includes signed linear filters, the Abstract and Contributions 2 and 4 should explicitly say "a symmetric *probability* mask" (or "a symmetric nonnegative mask of unit mass").

4. **"In $d \ge 2$ no kernel at a positive scale of an exact grid pyramid whose mask is carried by a lattice is rotation invariant" (Abstract, p. 1; Contribution 4, p. 4; Discussion, p. 105; Proposition 6.4(2), p. 85).**
   - *What is proved:* Proposition 6.4(2) proves that for an exact grid pyramid $(w, q)$ on a full-rank lattice $\Gamma \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ ($d \ge 2$) in the sense of Definition 6.3, no kernel $\mu_t = \mu_{0,t}$ ($t > 0$) is $SO(d)$-invariant, and if $\mathrm{supp}\,w$ spans $\mathbb{R}^d$, the orthogonal symmetry group of every $\mu_t$ is finite.
   - *Two points to tighten in the headline:*
     1. In Definition 6.3(c), discrete covariance is defined for a *scalar* dilation $D_q$ ($x \mapsto qx$, $q > 1$). As Section 7.1 (p. 105) and Open Problem 3 (p. 106) note, pyramids whose dilation matrix also rotates the lattice (such as Crowley's $\sqrt{2}$ pyramid or quincunx pyramids) are not covered by Definition 6.3. This scalar-dilation scope should be noted in Contribution 4 (p. 4).
     2. In the explanatory summary of Contribution 4 on p. 4, the text states: *"The Lévy measure of an infinitely divisible lattice law lies on the lattice, so the kernels of the family have their jumps on countably many points, and in $d \ge 2$ a rotation-invariant law with a nonzero Lévy measure cannot."* This explanation omits why the kernel cannot simply have a *zero* Lévy measure (i.e., be purely Gaussian): as explained later on p. 82 and proved in Lemma 3.7 and Proposition 6.4(1), an infinitely divisible law carried by a lattice has *no Gaussian part* ($A_w = 0$), which forces $A_t = 0$ and $\nu_t \neq 0$ for every $t > 0$.

5. **"The paper's own numbered statements have formal counterparts in Lean 4, machine-checked in all but one step, from ten cited facts taken as axioms" (Abstract, p. 1) vs. Section 1.1 (pp. 5–6) and Appendix A (pp. 106–108).**
   - *What is true:* The statement is accurate once "the paper's own numbered statements" is understood to mean all numbered statements of Sections 3, 4, 5, and 6 and the Supplement (as opposed to the cited background theorems in Section 2, of which 10 are taken as 15 axioms in Lean, 11 are proved in Lean, and 6—Theorems 2.7, 2.12, 2.13, 2.14, 2.30, and 2.31—are stated for the reader's orientation and neither imported nor proved in the formal development). To prevent any misreading of "the paper's own numbered statements", the Abstract should say "The numbered statements of Sections 3–6 and the supplement".

## 3. Mathematical Correctness

I have checked the statements and proofs across the main paper and the supplement. The mathematical arguments are rigorous and I found **no false theorem or fatal gap** in any proof. Below is a detailed assessment of the non-machine-checked parts, the key mathematical threads, the ten admitted cited facts, and a few minor points in the text.

### 3.1 What Section 1.1 states is not machine-checked
1. **The one unformalized step of Proposition 4.5(4) (p. 44; proof on p. 46):**
   - The unformalized step is the verification that the general binomial masks $w(\{hn\}) = 4^{-k}\binom{2k}{k+n}$ ($|n| \le k$, $k \ge 1$, $k \neq 2$) and the renormalized integer samples $\beta(n)$ of a centered B-spline $\beta$ of degree $r \ge 2$ with unit knot spacing are symmetric probability laws on $h\mathbb{Z}$ of bounded support other than $\delta_0$ (for $k = 2$, $\frac{1}{16}[1,4,6,4,1]$, this is machine-checked, as is the general implication that no symmetric mask of bounded support has an exact grid pyramid).
   - The printed argument on p. 46 is completely correct: for $k \ge 1$, $\binom{2k}{k+n} = \binom{2k}{k-n}$ is positive on $|n| \le k$ (including $\pm 1$), vanishes for $|n| > k$, and sums to $4^k$; and the centered B-spline of degree $r \ge 2$ (order $r+1 \ge 3$) is even and strictly positive on $(-(r+1)/2, (r+1)/2) \supseteq [-1, 1]$, so its restriction to $\mathbb{Z}$ has $2\lfloor r/2\rfloor + 1$ nonzero weights, including $0$ and $\pm 1$.
2. **The six unimported background theorems in Section 2 (Theorems 2.7, 2.12, 2.13, 2.14, 2.30, 2.31):**
   - None of these six theorems is cited inside the proof of any numbered statement in Sections 3–6 or the supplement; they are used only in the surrounding discussion. Their statements on pp. 9, 11, 12, 13, and 17 are mathematically accurate.
3. **The connecting prose:**
   - On **p. 69, top**, commenting on Proposition 5.11(3), the prose says: *"In clause (3) a density is fixed only almost everywhere, and the sample is read from the representative of Lemma 5.2(1), whose value at 0 is $+\infty$."* Because Proposition 5.11(3) proves that $\lim_{x \to 0} r^{-d}G(x/r) = +\infty$ (logarithmically in $d=2$ and as $|x|^{2-d}$ in $d \ge 3$), *no* representative of the density of $L_r$ is continuous and finite at $0$; point-sampling at the origin fails for every continuous version on $\mathbb{R}^d \setminus \{0\}$, not merely because of the choice of representative in Lemma 5.2(1).

### 3.2 Audit of the core mathematical arguments
- **The three nested strata and their strictness in $\mathbb{R}^d$ (Lemmas 3.3–3.9, Theorems 3.11 and 3.14, Propositions 3.12 and 3.13, pp. 20–28; Supplement Section S1, pp. S1–S7):**
  - Lemma 3.3 (p. 20) establishes infinite divisibility of every step $\mu_{s,t}$ from (I1) and (I2) alone via Khintchine's theorem on null arrays (Theorem 2.19), using a compactness argument on $\{0 \le u \le v \le T\}$ to obtain uniform tightness of short steps.
  - Lemma 3.4 (p. 20) uses symmetry and the non-vanishing of infinitely divisible transforms (Theorem 2.18) to deduce $\widehat{\mu}_{s,t} > 0$ and the real exponent $\Psi_t(\omega) = \langle \omega, A_t \omega\rangle + \int (1 - \cos\langle \omega, x\rangle)\nu_t(dx)$ with $A_s \le A_t$ and $\nu_s \le \nu_t$.
  - Lemma 3.5 (p. 21) proves that the covariance ratios of a symmetric family form a closed subgroup of $((0,\infty), \times)$, hence $\{1\}$, $q^{\mathbb{Z}}$ ($q > 1$), or $(0,\infty)$, and constructs the relabelling $\chi$ to the canonical discrete gauge $S_q t = qt$ (unique up to $\mathcal{G}_q$).
  - Lemma 3.8 (p. 23) and Lemma 3.9 (p. 24) characterize discrete covariance in Lévy coordinates ($A_{qt} = q^2 A_t$, $\nu_{qt} = D_q \nu_t$) and synthesize a $q$-covariant family from data on $[1, q]$. Note that in Lemma 3.9 (p. 25), subtracting ordered $\sigma$-finite Lévy measures $\nu_s \le \nu_t$ is handled carefully by partitioning $\mathbb{R}^d \setminus \{0\}$ into annuli $E_n$ on each of which $\nu_s$ and $\nu_t$ are finite.
  - Propositions 3.12 and 3.13 (pp. 26–28) and Theorem 3.14 (p. 28) separate the three isotropic strata cleanly: Witness 1 ($\nu_t = t\sigma_h$) fails $D_q \nu_t \ge \nu_t$ for all $q > 1$ because $D_q \sigma_h = \sigma_{qh}$ puts zero mass on the sphere of radius $h$ (Theorem 2.22); Witness 2 ($\nu_{q^\theta} = \sum_{i \in \mathbb{Z}} [(1-\theta)w_i + \theta w_{i-1}]\sigma_{hq^i}$) is $q$-covariant, but cannot be fully covariant because a self-decomposable law has a radially absolutely continuous Lévy measure $k_\xi(r)\,dr/r$ (Theorem 2.23) that assigns mass zero to every sphere $\{|x| = hq^i\}$.

- **What does not lift from the line (Proposition 3.16, p. 29) and strict shrinking of the isotropic classes of one ratio with dimension (Proposition 3.17, p. 30):**
  - Proposition 3.16 disintegrates a rotation-invariant Lévy measure $\nu$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ ($d \ge 2$) over spheres $\sigma_r$ and shows that its folded marginal $\varpi$ along any unit vector $e$ is $\int_{(0,\infty)} D_r \beta_d\,\overline{\nu}(dr)$, where $\beta_d$ is the law of $|\eta_1|$ under the uniform measure on $S^{d-1}$. Since $d \ge 2$, $\beta_d$ is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}$, so $\varpi$ is absolutely continuous and has no atoms.
  - Proposition 3.17 proves $\mathcal{A}_{d+1}^{(q)} \subsetneq \mathcal{A}_d^{(q)}$ for every $d \ge 1$ and $q > 1$ using the spherical-train witness $\nu_1 = \sum_{i \le 1} \sigma_{q^i}$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$, which puts mass $1$ on the sphere $q S^{d-1} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$. If its radial exponent lifted to a rotation-invariant semi-selfdecomposable law on $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$ with Lévy measure $\nu'$, projection onto $\mathbb{R}^d$ would require $\nu'(P^{-1}(q S^{d-1})) \ge 1$, whereas on every sphere of radius $r$ in $\mathbb{R}^{d+1}$ the cylinder $\{r\eta : r|P\eta| = q\}$ has $\eta_1^2 = 1 - q^2/r^2$ (at most two latitudes, a set of $\sigma_1'$-measure $0$), giving $\nu'(P^{-1}(q S^{d-1})) = 0$.

- **The ladder and the knot kernel (Proposition 3.19, pp. 32–36):**
  - The equivalence between exactness of the ladder of an infinitely divisible symmetric step $\varrho \neq \delta_0$, convergence of the downward equivalent weighting product $\prod_{m \ge 1} \widehat{\varrho}(q^{-m}\omega)$, the Lévy-measure integrability $\int (1 \wedge |x|^2)\nu^*(dx) < \infty$ of $\nu^* = \sum_{m \ge 1} D_{q^{-m}}\nu_\varrho$, and the logarithmic moment $\int \log_+ |x|\,\nu_\varrho(dx) < \infty \iff \int \log_+ |x|\,\varrho(dx) < \infty$ (via Theorem 2.26 with $g(x) = \log(|x| \vee e)$ from Theorem 2.27) is completely solid.
  - The proof in Clause (2) (p. 34) that the uniform coupling and the split coupling (when $\nu_\varrho = \nu_a + \nu_b$ on disjoint sets $B_a, B_b$) are not related by any gauge $g \in \mathcal{G}_q$, and the proof in Clause (3) (pp. 34–35) via Steinhaus's theorem that without the logarithmic moment the partial products tend to $0$ almost everywhere and cannot converge to a characteristic function, are both sound.

- **The stage lemma (Proposition 5.18, pp. 76–78) and the selection theorem (Theorem 5.19, pp. 78–80):**
  - The proof of Proposition 5.18(3) (p. 77) is a highlight of the paper. Testing the implicit-Euler maximum principle on finite cosine sums multiplied by $e^{-s|x|^2}$ as $s \downarrow 0$ and using a 4th-order Taylor bound on a small ball $|x| \le r$ yields the linear inequality $\sum_k a_k \rho(w_k) \le \sum_k a_k$ whenever $\sum_k a_k \rho(w_k)\langle w_k, x\rangle^2 \ge c|x|^2$ ($c > 0$). From this, Step 2 rules out zeros of $\rho = \widehat{\mu}$, and Step 3 applies the polarization identity to $m(\omega) = 1/\rho(\omega) - 1$ to conclude $m(\omega) = \omega \cdot C \omega$ with $C \ge 0$ without assuming infinite divisibility or any smoothness of $\mu$.
  - Theorem 5.19 (pp. 78–80) then deduces that requiring the principle at every octave stage $\mu_{t, qt}$ ($t \in [1, q)$) of an isotropic $q$-covariant family in the canonical discrete gauge forces $\mu_{t, qt} = L_{\chi(t)}$ and $\Psi_t = F_q^1(\chi(t)\,\cdot)$ with $\chi \in \mathcal{G}_q$.

- **Creation of local maxima in the plane (Proposition 5.15, pp. 71–73) and on the grid (Proposition 5.16, p. 74; Propositions 6.10 and 6.11, pp. 95–97):**
  - In Proposition 5.15(1), the witness $f_{a,\delta}$ (two Gaussian bumps at $\pm a e_1$ connected by a ridge of width $\delta$ in $x' \in \mathbb{R}^{d-1}$) has a unique local maximum at $0$ for all $a > 1, \delta > 0$. Because $d \ge 2$, the ridge $\rho_\delta$ satisfies $\|\rho_\delta\|_1 \to 0$ as $\delta \downarrow 0$, so $\|\mu * \rho_\delta\|_\infty \to 0$ for any absolutely continuous $\mu$, allowing the two smoothed bumps at $\pm a e_1$ to separate into at least two local maxima.
  - In Proposition 5.15(3), the subquadratic growth bound $F_q(2\pi s|\xi|) \le \varepsilon|\xi|^2 + 2N^2 b/\varepsilon$ and Faà di Bruno bounds on the derivatives of $F_q$ allow $f_{a,\delta} = N(0, \tau I) * g_0$ to be deconvolved back to scale $s$ in $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^d)$, proving that local maxima can be created at *every* rung of the Matérn and frame Matérn ladders.
  - On the grid $h\mathbb{Z}^d$ ($d \ge 2$, $q \ge 2$ integer), Proposition 5.16(3) and Proposition 6.10 invert infinitely divisible masks in $\ell^1(\mathbb{Z}^d)$ via $\widehat{w}^{-1} = e^M e^{-\widehat{a}}$, while Proposition 6.11 handles the non-infinitely-divisible binomial mask $\beta^{\otimes d}$ by observing that its decimated subsample $S(m) = w(\{qhm\})$ on $qh\mathbb{Z}^d$ is the tensor power of $\frac{1}{16}(\delta_{-1} + 6\delta_0 + \delta_1)$ (for $q=2$) or $\frac{6}{16}\delta_0$ (for $q \ge 3$), which is diagonally dominant and invertible in $\ell^1(\mathbb{Z}^d)$.

- **The lattice argument (Lemma 3.7, p. 22) and the obstruction to isotropy in $d \ge 2$ (Proposition 6.4, pp. 85–88):**
  - Lemma 3.7 projects an infinitely divisible law carried by a full-rank lattice $\Gamma \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ onto the dual basis coordinates $c_i(x) = \langle v_i, x\rangle$ and applies Sato's 1D lattice theorem (Theorem 2.25) to each coordinate to conclude $A^{\mathrm{S}} = 0$ and that $\nu$ is finite and carried by $\Gamma \setminus \{0\}$.
  - Proposition 6.4(1)–(2) shows that in an exact grid pyramid on $\Gamma$, every kernel $\mu_t$ ($t > 0$) has $A_t = 0$ and a nonzero Lévy measure $\nu_t$ ($D_{q^n}\nu_1 \le \nu_t \le D_{q^{n+1}}\nu_1$) carried by the countable set $\Gamma_q = \bigcup_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} q^k \Gamma$. Therefore $\nu_t$ has at least one nonzero atom $x$, and $SO(d)$-invariance in $d \ge 2$ would force the entire uncountable sphere $\{|y| = |x|\}$ to consist of atoms of $\nu_t$ inside $\Gamma_q$, a contradiction. Note that Proposition 6.4(1) does not even need to assume that $\mu_{s,t}$ is symmetric for intermediate scales $s, t \notin q^{\mathbb{Z}}$: symmetry of $\mu_1$ follows from $\widehat{\mu}_1(\omega) = \prod_{m \ge 1} \widehat{w}(q^{-m}\omega)$ being real, and the additivity of generating triplets under convolution $\mu_{q^{n+1}} = \mu_t * \mu_{t, q^{n+1}}$ and $\mu_t = \mu_{q^n} * \mu_{q^n, t}$ gives $A_t^{\mathrm{S}} = 0$ and $D_{q^n}\nu_1 \le \nu_t \le D_{q^{n+1}}\nu_1$ directly from Theorem 2.17(ii).

## 4. The Numerical Examples

1. **Table 1 and Figure 3 (p. 52, `scripts/grid_pyramid_exactness.py`):**
   - Proposition 4.7(2) proves analytically that for an integer ratio $q \ge 2$, the decimated forward–backward recursion $U_n$ on $q^n h\mathbb{Z}$ equals the renormalized sampled continuum step $V_n$ at level $n = 1$ ($U_1 = V_1$) and differs from it at every level $n \ge 2$ ($U_n \neq V_n$). Table 1 complements this qualitative theorem by measuring the total-variation distance $\frac{1}{2}\|U_n - V_n\|_{\ell^1}$ at $q = 2$, $n = 2, 3, 4$, and $h/(ct) \in \{1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1\}$. The numbers show roughly quadratic decay $O((h/ct)^2)$ as the grid refines relative to the scale, and the text carefully notes that no analytical error bound is proved.
   - *Reproducibility detail missing:* Both $U_n$ and $V_n$ have infinite support on $q^n h\mathbb{Z}$ (with closed-form coefficients given in the proof on p. 51). The caption of Table 1 should state the summation window $|m| \le M$ (or analytic tail formula) used to evaluate the $\ell^1$ distance.

2. **Sampled Gaussian increments (pp. 52–53, `scripts/sampled_gaussian_increments.py`):**
   - The text computes the inverse DFT of the ratio $\widehat{\lambda}_t(\vartheta)/\widehat{\lambda}_s(\vartheta)$ of two renormalized sampled Gaussians on a periodic grid of $4096$ points for four pairs of standard deviations in grid units: $(0.5, 0.6)$, $(1.0, 1.2)$, $(2.0, 2.5)$, and $(0.7, 3.0)$, finding minimum coefficients $-0.011$, $-0.0046$, $-2.8 \times 10^{-7}$, and $-2 \times 10^{-17}$.
   - *Design and interpretation:* For $(\sigma_s, \sigma_t) = (0.5, 0.6)$ and $(1.0, 1.2)$, the negative values $-0.011$ and $-0.0046$ are 13 orders of magnitude above double-precision rounding error and periodization tails ($\sim e^{-2048^2/3}$), so they convincingly demonstrate that renormalized sampled Gaussians do not form a nonnegative hemigroup on $h\mathbb{Z}$. For completeness, the text on p. 53 should state the sampling window used to construct the periodized Gaussians on the $4096$-point ring and the lattice index $n$ at which the minimum negative coefficient occurs.

3. **Figures 2, 4, and 5 (pp. 36, 73, 89):**
   - The figures directly illustrate the theoretical results (Proposition 3.13 / Theorem 3.14 in Figure 2; Proposition 5.15(1) in Figure 4; Proposition 6.4(3) and Proposition 6.7(4) in Figure 5).
   - *Reproducibility details missing in captions:* In Figure 4 (p. 73), the caption states that $L_r * f$ is *"computed by the transform $(1 + r^2|\omega|^2)^{-1}$ on a periodic grid"*, but omits the spatial domain size $[-L, L]^2$ and grid resolution $N \times N$. In Figure 5 (p. 89, middle panel), the caption plots the atoms of the Lévy measure $\nu_w = \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{p^k}{k}\sigma^{*k}|_{\Gamma\setminus\{0\}}$ of the five-point resolvent mask at $p = 4/5$ on $|x_i| \le 6h$, but does not state the truncation order $k_{\max}$ (or FFT size) used to evaluate the series in $k$.

## 5. The Trust Base

## 6. Novelty and Relation to Prior Work

The paper's positioning against the image-processing, scale-space, and probability literature is generally thorough and fair:
- **Burt (1981) [3], Burt & Adelson (1983) [2], and Meer, Baugher & Rosenfeld (1987) [25]:** Properly credited for single-step generating kernel constraints, equivalent weighting functions, and single-decimation frequency-domain optimization. The paper's reading of the downward limit $\lim_{M \to \infty} D_{q^{-1}}w * \dots * D_{q^{-M}}w$ as the knot kernel $\mu_1$ of a $q$-covariant hemigroup (Proposition 3.19) is a clean conceptual bridge.
  - *One attribution to clarify:* In Section 3.5 (pp. 37–38) and the Supplement (pp. S35, S40–S41), the convergence of the iterated dyadic equivalent weights of the binomial mask $\frac{1}{16}[1,4,6,4,1]$ to the centered cubic B-spline (with excess kurtosis $-3/10$, hence non-Gaussian) is presented in a way that could be misread as a new mathematical finding. While contrasting it with Burt and Adelson's informal Gaussian comparison [2, pp. 533–534] and with infinite divisibility is useful, the convergence of dyadic binomial subdivision to B-splines is a standard textbook fact in stationary subdivision and spline wavelets (Cavaretta, Dahmen & Micchelli [4]; Unser, Aldroubi & Eden [31]), and should be explicitly labeled as classical where Propositions 3.22(2), 6.13, and S.38 are introduced.
- **Lindeberg (1990, 1994, 2016) [18, 19, 20] and Lifshitz & Pizer / Damon [7]:** Properly credited. Placing Lindeberg's 1990 discrete scale space strictly in Stratum 1 (Proposition 3.12, Proposition S.5) and connecting the Matérn pyramid exponent $F_q(r) = \sum_{i \ge 1}\log(1 + c^2 q^{-2i}r^2)$ to Lindeberg's 2016 time-causal limit kernel [20, Sect. 5, (38), p. 62] via subordination (p. 62, p. S38) are both insightful and accurately attributed.
- **Semi-selfdecomposable laws (Maejima & Naito 1998 [23]; Sato 1999 [28, §15]) and semi-selfsimilar processes (Maejima & Sato 1999 [Supplement ref. 11]):**
  - *Missing citation in the main paper:* In the Supplement (p. S10, after Theorem S.10), the author notes that the bijection between $q^{\mathbb{Z}}$-covariant families and paths of exponents on one octave $[1, q]$ is the scale-space counterpart of Maejima and Sato's construction of semi-selfsimilar additive processes from one period [Supplement ref. 11, Thm. 7, p. 361]. However, in Section 3.2 of the main paper (Lemmas 3.8 and 3.9, pp. 23–25), where the exact same annulus parametrization and synthesis are proved in $\mathbb{R}^d$, Maejima and Sato (1999) is not cited (and is absent from the main paper's bibliography). It should be cited in Section 3.2 right after Lemma 3.9.
- **Nonstationary iterated Tikhonov regularization (Hanke & Groetsch 1998 [15]), diffusion reading (Radmoser, Scherzer & Weickert 2000 [27]), and edge-preserving decompositions (Farbman et al. 2008 [14]):** Appropriately credited on pp. 4 and 105 for geometric sequences of implicit Euler / Helmholtz steps; the author clearly states that what is new here is the embedding into a fully covariant continuum hemigroup, decimation in a pyramid, and the axiomatic selection via the implicit-Euler maximum principle (Theorem 5.19).
- **Multiresolution analysis (Mallat 1989 [24]) and Matérn covariances / SPDEs (Whittle 1954 [34]; Stein 1970 [30]; Lindgren, Rue & Lindström 2011 [22]):** Properly credited and contrasted on pp. 4, 55, 102, and S43.

## 7. Exposition and Structure

### 7.1 Forward references and definitions used before they are given
1. **A 40-page forward reference for "exact in sense (i)/(ii)/(iii)" and "grid family":**
   Section 4.2 (p. 43) and Proposition 4.7(2) (p. 49) rely formally on the terminology *"exact in sense (i)"*, *"exact in sense (ii) of Definition 6.2"*, *"exact in sense (iii)"*, and *"grid family"*, which are not defined until Definitions 6.1 and 6.2 on pp. 83–84. Moving Definitions 6.1 and 6.2 to the start of Section 4.2 (or Section 3.4) will eliminate this 40-page forward jump.
2. **Forward references within Section 6 to Section 6.4:**
   Definition 6.1 (p. 83), Proposition 6.7(4) (p. 93), and Proposition 6.10(1) (p. 95) use the $(2d+1)$-point discrete Helmholtz resolvent $\kappa_t$ and the nine-point resolvent $\kappa_t^9$, which are not defined until Propositions 6.15 and 6.18 in Section 6.4 (pp. 101–103). Section 6.4 (or the definitions of $\Delta_h, \kappa_t, \Delta_9, \kappa_t^9$) should be placed at the beginning of Section 6 before Definition 6.1.
3. **Proofs in the main paper that depend on the Supplement:**
   The main paper claims to be readable on its own, yet the proof of Proposition 4.5 (pp. 44–45) cites Lemmas S.1 and S.3, and the proof of the flagship Matérn pyramid theorem in $\mathbb{R}^d$, **Theorem 5.4** (pp. 59–62, as well as Propositions 5.8(3), 5.9(1), and 5.14), depends on Lemma S.34, Theorem S.35, Theorem S.36, and Proposition S.37 from the Supplement. In fact, the 1D analytical estimates in Theorem S.35(3)–(4) (the generator symbol bound and the $q \downarrow 1$ dilogarithm limit) are re-proved in $d$ dimensions in Proposition 5.11(1) (p. 68) and Theorem 5.4(5) (p. 62), and Lemma S.34(1) is the $d=1$ case of Lemma 5.2(2) (p. 56). Making the proof of Theorem 5.4 in the main paper self-contained requires only folding the 1D Pólya-frequency / ladder-counting step (Theorem S.36 and Proposition S.37) or its citation to Theorem 2.15/2.16 directly into Section 5.1, and deriving Proposition 4.5 as the $d = 1$ corollary of Section 6 (or citing Section 3 directly instead of Lemmas S.1 and S.3).

### 7.2 Length, duplication, and what a journal version should lose
At **112 pages** (plus **46 pages** of supplement), the manuscript is roughly three times the length of a standard mathematical-imaging journal paper. Crucially, almost no mathematical result needs to be sacrificed to bring the main paper down to **45–55 pages**: the excess length comes from repeating the same theorems and proofs multiple times across $d = 1$ and $d \ge 1$ and from transcribing verbose Lean proof scripts into prose. Specifically, a journal version should cut:
1. **Duplicate $d=1$ vs. $d \ge 1$ grid-pyramid theorems (saves ~10 pages):** Proposition 4.5 (pp. 43–46, grid pyramids on $h\mathbb{Z}$) is the $d = 1$ special case of Propositions 6.4, 6.6, and 6.13 (pp. 85–99, grid pyramids on a full-rank lattice $\Gamma \subset \mathbb{R}^d$), and Proposition 6.12(1) (pp. 97–98) spends another page proving that Definitions 4.4 and 6.3 coincide at $d = 1$. State the exactness criterion once for general $d \ge 1$ (or make Proposition 4.5 a short 1-page specialization of the general theorem without repeating the 3-page proof).
2. **Quadruple repetition of the uniform and split couplings (saves ~4 pages):** The uniform and split coupling constructions on $[1, q]$ and the proof that they are not related by any gauge $g \in \mathcal{G}_q$ are written out in Proposition 3.19(2) (pp. 33–34), repeated in Proposition 4.5(2) (p. 45), repeated at full length in Theorem 5.19(1) (pp. 79–80), and repeated again in Proposition 6.6(2) (p. 92). Prove this once in Proposition 3.19(2) and invoke it by reference in the other three places (in Theorem 5.19(1), simply note that the rotation-invariant Lévy measure $\nu$ of $L_{\sqrt{\tau}}$ splits into $\nu|_{\{|x|\le 1\}} + \nu|_{\{|x|>1\}}$ and satisfies $\int \log_+ |x|\,\nu(dx) < \infty$).
3. **Triple repetition of the cubic B-spline limit of the binomial mask (saves ~3 pages):** Viète's product $\prod_{m \ge 1}\cos(2^{-m}x) = \mathrm{sinc}\,x$ and the convergence of the binomial mask to the cubic B-spline are proved in Proposition 3.22(2) (p. 38), proved again in Proposition 6.13 (p. 99), and proved a third time in Proposition S.38(3) (p. S41). Prove it once in Proposition 3.22(2) and cite it in Section 6.3 and the Supplement.
4. **Repetition of the 1D strata between Section 3, Section 4.1, and Supplement Section S1 (saves ~4 pages in main paper, ~7 pages in supplement):** Since $O(1) = \{\pm 1\}$, symmetric families on $\mathbb{R}$ are isotropic families in dimension $1$ via $x \mapsto xe_1$ (Lemma 4.2). Section 4.1 (pp. 39–42) can be condensed to Lemma 4.2 and a 1-page summary of folded data $(a(t), \varpi_t)$, while Section S1 of the supplement (pp. S1–S8) need only state the folded-data dictionary rather than re-proving Lemmas 3.5–3.8, Theorem 3.11, Propositions 3.12–3.13, Theorem 3.14, and Proposition 3.18 from scratch.
5. **Theorem-prover boilerplate inside proofs (saves ~15–20 pages):** In nearly every proof in Sections 3, 5, and 6, the text re-derives from Theorem 2.17 that a symmetric infinitely divisible law has drift $0$ and symmetric Lévy measure because the odd compensator integrates to $0$, re-proves that a finite measure is determined by its Fourier transform by integrating against Gaussians or smooth bump functions $\Phi_n$, and ends every proof with the sentence *"Every step is machine-checked, and the cited facts are..."* (which duplicates Table 4). Establishing the symmetric Lévy–Khintchine data $(A, \nu)$ and Fourier uniqueness once in Lemma 3.4 and omitting the per-proof verification footers will make the proofs far crisper and easier to read.
