## Probability

**1. Sato 1999 (`sato1999levy`, held).**
- a. Yes. Thm 9.1 (p. 47): "for every t, the distribution of X_t is infinitely divisible"; proof p. 51 by Lemma 9.6 (the increments
  X(kt/n) - X((k-1)t/n) form a null array) and Khintchine Thm 9.3 (p. 47). Thm 9.7(i) (p. 51): each mu_{s,t} is infinitely
  divisible, with (9.13)-(9.16); proof (pp. 51-52) applies Thm 9.1 to X_{s+.} - X_s.
- b. Yes, p. 52. Thm 9.8(i): (1) A_0 = 0, nu_0 = 0, gamma(0) = 0; (2) A_s <= A_t (quadratic-form order) and nu_s(B) <= nu_t(B)
  for s < t; (3) continuity as s -> t (A_s -> A_t, nu_s(B) -> nu_t(B) for B inside {|x| > eps}, gamma(s) -> gamma(t)).
  Thm 9.8(ii) is the converse (existence, unique up to identity in law). gamma(t) is continuous, not monotone.
- c. Thm 16.1, pp. 99-100. (i) a broad-sense selfsimilar additive process has selfdecomposable X_t for every t. (ii) every
  non-trivial selfdecomposable mu and every H > 0 give, uniquely in law, a non-trivial H-selfsimilar additive process with
  X_1 ~ mu. "Exactly" holds for non-trivial laws (trivial laws are selfdecomposable by the proof of (i)).
- d. Yes. Thm 19.2, p. 120, is stated for "an additive process on R^d" with system of triplets; the intensity measure on
  H = (0,inf) x (R^d minus {0}) is nu~((0,t] x B) = nu_t(B); J is a Poisson random measure with that intensity (i); (19.2) holds
  with uniform convergence on bounded t-intervals. Def 19.1 (Poisson random measure) p. 119; Thm 19.3 (integrable jumps) p. 121.
- e. Cor 24.6, p. 149, as printed: "S_mu in Z iff A = 0, S_nu in Z, and the drift gamma_0 is in Z". The statement does NOT say
  nu is finite. Finiteness is in the proof (nu(R) < inf, by contradiction) and the closing note "S_mu in Z implies that the
  total mass of nu is finite".
- f. Thm 27.4 (Continuity), p. 175: for a Levy process with triplet (A,nu,gamma) these are equivalent: mu_{X_t} continuous for
  every t > 0; for some t > 0; type B or C, i.e. "A != 0 or nu(R^d) = inf". Your belief is right. Hartman-Wintner: Thm 27.16,
  p. 182: A = 0 and nu discrete with infinite total mass => mu absolutely continuous or continuous singular (proof p. 183 via
  Prop 27.17 and Prop 27.18). Thm 27.15 (Wolfe), p. 181: a non-trivial semi-selfdecomposable law is absolutely continuous or
  continuous singular.
- g. Thm 17.5, pp. 108-109. (i) if rho satisfies (17.11), integral over |x| > 2 of log|x| rho(dx) < inf, the OU-type process
  generated by (G, rho, 0, c) has a limit distribution, transform exp of integral psi(e^{-cs}z) ds (17.12), selfdecomposable.
  (ii) every selfdecomposable law arises from a unique triplet (G, rho, beta) satisfying (17.11). The log-moment is on the
  background Levy measure rho, not on the nu of the limit (related by (17.14)). Without (17.11): Thm 17.11.

**3. Maejima-Sato 1999 (`maejima1999semi`).** A dichotomy, not a trichotomy: Thm 1(ii), p. 349. For a non-trivial, stochastically
continuous wide-sense semi-selfsimilar process let F be the set of a > 0 admitting (b, c(t)) in (1.3); F meets (1, inf);
a_0 := inf(F inter (1,inf)); if a_0 > 1 then F = {a_0^n : n in Z}; if a_0 = 1 then F = (0, inf) (selfsimilar). "{1}" is not a
case (F always contains 1; non-triviality forces F to meet (1,inf)). Proof via log F a closed additive subgroup of R:
pp. 350-351. Epoch = any a in F inter (1,inf) (Def 3, p. 350). Thm 13, p. 368: Lamperti transform to periodically stationary.

**4. Maejima-Sato 2003 (`maejima2003semi`).** J. Math. Kyoto Univ. 43 (2003) 609-639 (the Zotero container reads "Kyoto Journal of
Mathematics", DOI 10.1215/kjm/1250283698). Yes to the log-time passage: Thm 1.1, p. 611: for a Q-semi-selfsimilar natural
additive process X with epoch a, p = log a, Z_s = e^{-sQ} X_{e^s} (1.4) and Lambda(B) = integral over exp B of t^{-Q} dX_t
(1.5); Lambda is an independently scattered random measure periodic with period p, finite log-moment; Z is the unique
semi-stationary (= periodically stationary, defined p. 611) OU-type process. Thm 1.2, p. 612: converse X_t = t^Q Z_{log t}.
Thm 1.3, p. 612: the law of X_1 is (a^{-1}, Q)-decomposable. Precision: the periodic object is the random measure Lambda (increments
with period-p laws); Z is periodically stationary. Semi-Levy process (period p): defined p. 610.

**5. Becker-Kern 2003 (`becker-kern2003stable`, held).** Exists: P. Becker-Kern, "Stable and Semistable Hemigroups: Domains of
Attraction and Self-Decomposability", J. Theoret. Probab. 16(3) (2003), from p. 573, DOI 10.1023/a:1025664314657. Uses the word
throughout (title, abstract, keywords); p. 573: "H = {m_{s,t} | 0 <= s <= t} a (convolution) hemigroup", m_{s,r} * m_{r,t} =
m_{s,t}. p. 574: D(H) = {B in GL(R^d): B m_{s,t} = m_{ls,lt} for some l > 0}, f(B) = l, and three cases of Im(f): {1} (trivial),
all of (0,inf) (stable hemigroup), c^Z for some c > 1 ((B,c)-semistable hemigroup). So both the one-ratio case and every dilation
are treated. The relation is B m_{s,t} = m_{ls,lt} with an operator B, not literally D_q mu_{s,t} = mu_{qs,qt} (the scalar
case is the special case; my reading). Exponents: p. 575; domain of attraction Thm 2.1, p. 576.

**6. Wolfe 1982 and Vervaat 1979 (both held).** Vervaat: yes. Thm 1.6(b), p. 757: for iid pairs (A_n, B_n) with
mu = E log|A| in (-inf, 0), (1.2) has a solution iff E log_+|B| < inf, and the series (1.7) converges and solves (1.2). Lemma 1.7
(p. 757): the series converges with probability 1 iff E log_+|B| < inf. Cor 1.9: convergence in distribution iff a.s. With
A = c constant in (0,1): sum c^n B_n converges a.s. iff E log_+|B| < inf; no symmetry, no infinite divisibility; B arbitrary
iid; on R. Wolfe: NOT this statement. Thm 1, p. 306, is for the continuous-time X(t) driven by B(t) with independent homogeneous
increments: converges in law iff E log_+|B(1)| < inf, limit of class L. His discrete remarks: p. 303 (the discrete process may
converge to a law not even infinitely divisible), p. 308 (limit of discrete (1.1) iff f(u) = f(au) f_a(u)). Cite Vervaat Thm 1.6(b)
and Lemma 1.7, p. 757, for the discrete criterion.

**8. Karlin 1968 (`karlin1968total`; printed p. 233 = pdf 245).** Both senses appear. The OUTPUT count S^-[g(x)] is pointwise:
Def 3.1, p. 230, S^-[f(t)] = sup of S^- over all finite t_1 < ... < t_m in T. The INPUT count is essential: S_mu(f) = number of
"relevant sign changes with respect to the sigma-finite positive measure mu" (Def 3.2, p. 231; null sets ignored). Thm 3.1(i),
p. 233: K sign-regular of order r, f with n <= r-1 relevant sign changes => S^-[g(x)] <= S_mu[f(y)] whenever S_mu(f) <= r-1.
Class: "The function f will represent a bounded Borel-measurable function unless stated explicitly to the contrary" (p. 233);
mu a fixed sigma-finite nonnegative measure; the integral of K dmu finite for each x. So the printed class is BOUNDED
Borel-measurable, not "integrable". Converse: Thm 4.2, pp. 242-243 (as V-selection p. 34 cites).

## Imaging and scale space

**9. Burt-Adelson and Burt.** Burt-Adelson (`burt1983laplacian`) p. 533: "equal contribution ... stipulates that all nodes at a
given level must contribute the same total weight (=1/4) to nodes at the next higher level"; for w = (c,b,a,b,c): a + 2c = 2b.
Ratio 2 only. Burt (`burt1981fast`) p. 23: (C4) sum_i w(j + ir) = 1/r for all 0 <= j < r, general order r. p. 24: it "insures that
every sample point of f(x) contributes with equal weight to every level of the HDC when we adopt a reduced form of
correlation", and "also insures convergence". Motivation: uniform weight of the samples under reduction (sampling), plus
convergence of the equivalent kernels; not aliasing ("alias" has zero hits in both).

**10. Meer-Baugher-Rosenfeld 1987 (`meer1987frequency`).** Criterion: closeness to the ideal rectangular low-pass (16), p. 513
(W = 1 for 0 <= theta_i < pi/2, 0 beyond; sinc kernel (17), infinite extent), for decimation by 2 (Fig. 1, p. 513). Judged by two
indexes, p. 513: antialiasing index eta (18), "the relative amount of spectrum folded back (aliased)", and filtering index mu
(19), deviation from the ideal in the passband; p. 514: an optimal kernel "maximally attenuates the input spectrum beyond half
the sampling frequency" and is maximally flat in the passband. It does not impose exact zeros at alias frequencies: it
measures integrated attenuation (only the ideal (16) is zero there). p. 515: "not possible to simultaneously obtain good
antialiasing and good low pass filtering" for even and odd kernels. Ratio 2 only.

**11. Lindeberg 1990 (`lindeberg1990scalespace`).**
- a. Yes, p. 246 (image checked). (42) dL/dt = C((1-gamma) nabla_5^2 L + gamma nabla_x^2 L), gamma in [0,1]; "we may set C to 1/2"
  to match the 1-D constant (28), so (44) dL/dt = (1/2)((1-gamma) nabla_5^2 L + gamma nabla_x^2 L). Thm 7, p. 245: alpha, beta >= 0
  necessary. p. 246: "If gamma = 1/3 we get the nine-point operator"; for large scales it gives the "most" isotropic second
  order approximation. But it is NOT chosen: "It is not clear that rotational invariance is a primary quality ... in the discrete
  case" and "We leave the question about definite selection of gamma open"; gamma = 0 "should not be a too bad choice".
  Same page: the explicit step kernel (46) is unimodal iff gamma <= 2/3, the Fourier transform (43) iff gamma <= 1/2;
  separable iff gamma = 0 (Prop 14); gamma = 1 splits into two uncoupled sublattices.
- b. Yes. The 1-D classification starts from "scale-space kernel" (no increase in local extrema, pp. 238-239); Thm 1, p. 239:
  a discrete kernel is a scale-space kernel iff its coefficients form a normalized Polya frequency sequence; Thm 2, pp. 239-240:
  generating-function form (Edrei-Schoenberg). Variation diminution is the theory of Schoenberg and Karlin, named p. 239 (proofs
  omitted). Two-point averages (1 + a z) are factors of the form, so covered (shown explicitly in the 1994 book, Q13).

**13. Lindeberg 1994 (`lindeberg1994scale`).**
- a. p. 101, Ch. 4 opening: "it is possible to show that there are no non-trivial kernels on R^2 or Z^2 with the property that
  they never introduce new local extrema". Not proved as a theorem: argued by the Lifshitz-Pizer counterexample (two hills joined
  by a ridge, quoted freely, Fig. 4.1), then "The same argument can be carried out in the discrete case ... Given an arbitrary
  non-trivial convolution kernel, it is always possible to create a counter-example". So: every non-trivial convolution
  kernel, continuous and discrete; cited and sketched; no theorem number.
- b. Thm 3.16, p. 80 (image checked): generating function c z^k exp(q_{-1}z^{-1} + q_1 z) prod (1 + alpha_i z)(1 + delta_i z^{-1}) /
  ((1 - beta_i z)(1 - gamma_i z^{-1})), "c > 0, k in Z, q_{-1}, q_1, alpha_i, beta_i, gamma_i, delta_i >= 0, beta_i, gamma_i < 1,
  sum (alpha_i + beta_i + gamma_i + delta_i) < inf". Cor 3.17 (same page): alpha_i, delta_i >= 0 (two-point averages), 0 <= beta_i,
  gamma_i < 1 (first-order recursive), plus diffusion smoothing, rescaling, translation.
- c. p. 82, Sect. 3.4.1, (3.39) L_i = K_{i<-i-1} * L_{i-1}: "Combined with a sub-sampling operator it provides a possible theoretical
  basis for pyramid representations"; footnote 7: for pyramids "the decreasing number of grid points, aliasing and the fixed scale
  sampling ... Those issues are not covered by this treatment". p. 122, Sect. 4.5.4: pyramids "imply a fixed relation between scale
  and resolution"; open question "to what extent a subsampling operator can be introduced in this representation while still
  maintaining the theoretical properties associated with having a continuous scale parameter".

**14. Scherzer-Weickert 2000 (`scherzer2000relations`).** The relevant text is Sect. 4 ("Lyapunov Functionals for Regularization
Methods"), pp. 50-53, not 3.2-6. For ONE TIKHONOV step: Lemma 6, p. 53: under (7)-(9) and g^ monotone (15), f in L^inf, "for any
h > 0 the regularized solution satisfies a <= u_h <= b" (a = ess inf f, b = ess sup f), proved by truncation min{b, u_h}: the
extremum principle for the minimizer u_h of (6), I(u) = ||u - f||^2 + h integral g^(|grad u|^2). The diffusion extremum principle
(4) is stated for the continuous solution (Thm 5, p. 50), not proved. Identification: p. 43 abstract: "Regularization may be regarded
as diffusion filtering with an implicit time discretization where one single step is used"; p. 44 (parameter = diffusion time);
p. 46 (5), exact for linear diffusion. So the maximum principle for an implicit step is established through the Tikhonov
functional (Lemma 6), not by a discrete maximum principle on the implicit scheme; the identification is a stated relation.

**15. Alvarez-Guichard-Lions-Morel 1993 (`alvarez1993axioms`).** Thm 2, p. 211 (Sect. 3.2 "Why a second-order partial differential
equation?"): under [Translation invariance], [Comparison principle], [Grey-scale-shift invariance], [Recursivity], [Regularity] and
[Locality] there is a continuous F on S^N x R^N with (T_t f - f)/t -> F(D^2 f, Df), F satisfying (4) (p. 210): F(A,p,t) >= F(B,p,t) for
A >= B, i.e. nondecreasing in the Hessian (degenerate ellipticity). (iii): T_t u_0 is the unique viscosity solution of
u_t = F(D^2 u, Du, t). Existence of a generator: Thm 1, p. 209. "Nonnegative characteristic form" is the reading of (4) for F
linear in A; the paper states monotonicity in A. [Locality] is what reduces the generator to (D^2 f, Df) (proof pp. 211-212).
